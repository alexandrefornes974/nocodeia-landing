# Brazil Company Database: Find B2B Clients With AI

A Brazil company database is a searchable list of Brazilian businesses built from the CNPJ registry, the national company register that Brazil's federal tax authority publishes as open data every month. It shows each company's industry code, size, address and partners, so a sales team in Brazil can target firms that look like its best customers.

This guide is for companies with a team, branch, partner or distributor in Brazil. With AI on top of that data, the team compares its customer base with the registry, gets companies similar to its best customers, sees who decides and improves with every suggestion it accepts or rejects.

If your Brazil team is small, you know the scene: the morning goes into building lists in a spreadsheet, half the contacts have nothing to do with your business and the list runs out before the target. The question is always the same: who do we call tomorrow? This guide shows how AI answers it, where the data comes from and how to stay within the LGPD, Brazil's data protection law.

## What is a CNPJ, and why does it matter for B2B sales in Brazil?

The CNPJ (Cadastro Nacional da Pessoa Jurídica) is the registration number that every formal business in Brazil has. Think of it as the company's ID card. It plays a role similar to an EIN in the United States, although the two are not legally equivalent.

For B2B sales, each CNPJ record says what the company does, how big it is and who its partners are. Two more Brazilian terms come up all the time:

- **CNAE:** the activity code that says what the company does, such as bakery, trucking company or clinic.
- **MEI (microempreendedor individual):** an individual micro-entrepreneur registered as a business. The company name often carries the owner's name, which matters for privacy (more on that below).

## Where does Brazil's company data come from?

It comes from the open CNPJ database, which Brazil's Federal Revenue Service (Receita Federal) publishes on the [Open Data Portal](https://dados.gov.br/dados/conjuntos-dados/cadastro-nacional-da-pessoa-juridica---cnpj) (in Portuguese), with monthly updates and a free license. Each record includes:

- **Main and secondary activities (CNAE):** what the company does.
- **Size, share capital and legal nature:** these separate a small shop from a manufacturer.
- **Opening date:** shows whether the company is new or long established.
- **Registered address, phone and email.**
- **Partners:** name and role of each person on the company's partner list.

When someone looks for a "Brazil company list" or a list of companies by CNAE, they are asking for a slice of this database. The data is free, but it comes in huge compressed files, and both the portal and the files are in Portuguese. Teams without technical staff usually use a tool that downloads, organizes and updates everything.

## How does AI pick the right companies to approach?

AI looks at what your best customers have in common, such as activity, size and state, and searches for companies with the same profile. That replaces the cold list, bought or mined by hand, with a short queue of companies that look like the ones already buying from you, with the reason behind each suggestion. Here is the difference:

| | Prospecting the old way | Prospecting with AI |
|---|---|---|
| Where the list comes from | Bought list, Google searches, referrals | Open CNPJ data matched against your customer base |
| Who chooses | The salesperson, on gut feeling | AI suggests and explains; the salesperson decides |
| How many at a time | Hundreds of names at once | A short queue, best matches first |
| How it improves | It doesn't improve on its own | It learns from every accept and reject |
| Who approves contact | Nobody, it just goes out | A person approves before any outreach |

The search also covers activities related to yours. A packaging supplier that sells well to bakeries, for example, also gets pastry shops and cookie factories in the region. Step by step:

1. **You share your customer base** or the sector you want to serve.
2. **AI matches it against the CNPJ database** and separates the similar companies.
3. **Each company gets a score** and the reason for it: same activity, same size, same region.
4. **AI shows who decides:** partners, directors or managers.
5. **You accept or reject**, and the next batch comes in sharper.

The salesperson stays in charge. The difference is that they stop searching and start choosing.

## What does Brazil's LGPD require when you prospect companies?

You can prospect Brazilian companies and stay within the LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados, Brazil's General Data Protection Law). Company data, such as legal name, CNPJ and activity, is not personal data. A partner's name or a director's email is, and it can be used under legitimate interest. That legal basis allows the use of data when it is necessary for a genuine interest of the company, as long as the person's rights are not harmed.

That calls for transparency and a right to object. The [LGPD](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm) (in Portuguese) sets out this basis in art. 7, item IX. The right way to do it:

1. **Use only what you need** for the outreach, as art. 10, paragraph 1 requires.
2. **Run the balancing test** described in the [ANPD guide on legitimate interest](https://www.gov.br/anpd/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/materiais-educativos-e-publicacoes/guia_legitimo_interesse.pdf) (in Portuguese; the ANPD is Brazil's data protection authority), in three phases: purpose, necessity, and balancing with safeguards.
3. **Say where the data came from and why** in the first contact (art. 10, paragraph 2).
4. **Offer an easy channel** for the person to object, unsubscribe and ask for deletion, as the ANPD guide's checklist asks. The right to object is in art. 18, paragraph 2.
5. **Keep a block list** so that anyone who asked to leave never hears from you again.

Be careful with MEIs: the company name often carries the owner's name, so that data is personal. ## FAQ

### What is a CNPJ number in Brazil?

It is the registration number of every formal business in Brazil. Its record, published as open data by the Federal Revenue Service, shows the company's activity (CNAE), size, registered address and partners.

### How do I get a list of Brazilian companies by industry?

Download the open CNPJ database from Brazil's Open Data Portal and filter by the CNAE code of the activity you want. Since the files are large, most companies use a tool that already does that filtering.

### Is there a free Brazil company list I can download?

Yes, the Federal Revenue database is free and open. The work lies in downloading, joining and cleaning huge files, and in keeping them updated every month. If you want the data already processed, Proacta CRM has a 14-day trial with no credit card, for companies with a Brazilian CNPJ.

### Does Brazil's LGPD apply to CNPJ data?

The LGPD protects data about people, not companies (art. 5, item I). Legal name and CNPJ fall outside it; a partner's name, or a person's email and phone, fall inside. For an MEI, the company name is often the owner's, so treat it as personal data.

### How many active companies are there in Brazil?

In Proacta CRM's November 20, 2026 load of the open CNPJ database, there are 28,654,371 active records, counting MEIs and branches. That is our own count over the open data, not an official figure published by the Federal Revenue Service.
