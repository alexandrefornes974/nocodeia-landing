AI for Small Business: Where to Start in Sales

AI for small business works best when you start with marketing and sales. See what the research says, which tasks to automate first and how to choose.

By Alexandre·September 30, 2026·6 min read

AI for small business means using artificial intelligence to take repetitive tasks off your team, like answering quote requests, following up with people who went quiet and organizing contacts. The best starting point is usually marketing and sales: it's the area most business owners planning to adopt AI point to, and in our experience, where results show up fastest.

You've probably heard you need to "bring AI into the business." The problem is nobody tells you where. So the company signs up for a tool, nobody really uses it, and the verdict becomes "AI doesn't work for my business." It does. But starting with the tool means starting at the end.

Where are businesses starting to use AI?

In marketing and sales. A survey by CNDL and SPC Brasil (Brazilian retail and credit organizations) with the research firm Offerwise, released in September 2026, shows that 69% of Brazilian business owners who plan to adopt AI see marketing and sales as the area with the most potential, according to CNDL (in Portuguese).

Read the table carefully: the 69% applies to owners who plan to adopt AI, not to every business. And the most telling number sits in the middle. Only 14% of businesses already use AI. If you start now, you still get there before most.

Why are marketing and sales the best place to start?

Because that's where the most repetitive, rule-based tasks live, and where results are easy to measure. You know how many quotes you answered this week and how many proposals never got a reply. If automation improves those numbers, you'll see it.

Business owners see it too. In the same survey, 52% see AI as a driver of higher sales and 41% believe it makes the company more competitive.

Compare that with finance or legal: there, a mistake is expensive and the rules have more exceptions. In sales, if a follow-up message comes out a little generic, the damage is small and you fix it the next week.

Which marketing and sales tasks can you automate first?

The ones that repeat every week and follow the same script. Automation, here, is simple: a repetitive task the system starts doing on its own, with nobody on the team copying and pasting.

  • Answering standard quote requests. The customer fills out the form and gets your price list and terms right away.
  • Following up on unanswered proposals. Three days later, the system sends the customer a reminder, and the salesperson only steps in when they reply.
  • Organizing contacts. Anyone who filled out your website form or sits in a loose spreadsheet goes straight into the CRM, the system where the company tracks customers and sales.
  • Drafting posts and emails. AI writes the first version and a person reviews it before it goes out.
  • Summarizing meetings. After a customer meeting, the system lists the next steps and creates the tasks.

Notice that none of these tasks takes the salesperson out of the conversation. They take the salesperson out of the spreadsheet.

How do you pick your first automation in 4 steps?

Pick the task that eats up the most team hours and has a clear rule. It's the same method we use in the free Nocodeia consultation before any workflow automation project:

  1. List the tasks that repeat every week. Ask everyone on the team to write down, for five days, anything they did more than three times.
  2. Measure the time and do the math. If someone spends 2 hours a day answering quote requests, that's 2 hours times 20 working days, or 40 hours a month. A full work week, every month.
  3. Choose the task with a clear rule and a cheap mistake. "Send the price list to anyone who asked for a quote" has a clear rule. "Negotiate a discount with a big customer" doesn't.
  4. Run a pilot and compare. Automate only that task, let it run for 1 to 3 weeks and compare before and after: hours spent, quotes answered, proposals that got a reply.

That's the logic behind the AI-powered B2B (business-to-business) prospecting engine and Proacta CRM we built (both among the projects we've delivered), a custom sales CRM with a LinkedIn prospecting agent: first the task that weighed the most, then the rest.

What usually goes wrong when a small business starts with the tool?

The most common mistake is buying the tool before knowing which task it will solve. CNDL (in Portuguese) itself names the lack of practical know-how about using the technology as the main obstacle for businesses.

The second mistake is putting AI in the wrong part of the customer conversation. In an Ipsos survey in Brazil reported by Mundo do Marketing (in Portuguese), only 31% of consumers who notice AI in customer service say it improved their experience, and 38% think companies gain more from AI than customers do.

The message is clear: use AI to take repetitive work off your team, and leave the relationship to the people who are good at relationships.

Automation or AI agent: which should you pick first?

For most small businesses, automation first. Automation follows a fixed rule ("if the customer asked for a quote, send the price list"), costs less and is more predictable. An AI agent holds the conversation and decides the next step on its own, and it makes sense when the volume of conversations is high.

FAQ

What is the best AI for small business?

There's no single best one for everyone. The best is the one that solves the task that eats up most of your team's time. Start by picking the task, using the 4-step method above, and only then pick the tool.

How do I use AI in my business?

Start with one repetitive marketing or sales task, like answering quote requests or following up on proposals. Measure how much time it takes, automate only that task, run a pilot for a few weeks and compare before and after.

How can AI help with sales?

It takes the repetitive work off the salesperson: answering standard quote requests, reminding customers about a proposal, organizing contacts in the CRM and summarizing meetings. That way the salesperson spends more time talking to people who are ready to buy.

What's the difference between workflow automation with and without AI?

Automation without AI follows fixed rules, like "if a form comes in, create the contact in the CRM." With AI, the system also understands free text, like sorting a customer's request or drafting a reply, and a person reviews it when needed.
